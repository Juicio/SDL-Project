Variables used in Council Bluffs Omaha comparison

  • Auto Regional Job Accessibility (D5AR): Total regional employment accessible within a 45-minute travel time by automobile from the census block group. Higher values indicate greater regional labor-market reach via personal vehicle.
  • Census Block Group Identifier (GEOID12): Unique 12-digit Federal Information Processing Standards (FIPS) code used to merge tabular Smart Location Database records with Census Bureau TIGRIS geographic polygon shapefiles.
  • Distance to Nearest Transit Stop (D4A): Network pedestrian walking distance (in meters) from the block group population centroid to the nearest fixed-route public transit stop. Values are capped at an upper threshold of 3,000 meters to limit the statistical distortion of unserved exurban areas while preserving rank isolation.
  • Gross Residential Density (D1A): Total housing units per acre of unprotected land. Higher values indicate denser residential settlement patterns capable of supporting frequent transit service.
  • Jurisdiction (jurisdiction): Binary geographic indicator distinguishing census block groups located in Douglas and Sarpy Counties, Nebraska (Omaha urbanized area) from those in Pottawattamie County, Iowa (Council Bluffs).
  • National Walkability Index (NatWalkInd): Composite index evaluating overall pedestrian friendliness based on employment and household diversity, intersection density, and transit proximity. Scale: \(1 =\) Least Walkable (Auto-Dependent Car Suburb); \(20 =\) Most Walkable (Pedestrian-Oriented Urban Core).
  • Percentage of Zero-Car Households (Pct_ZeroCar): Proportion of occupied housing units within the block group that do not own or have access to a personal passenger vehicle, derived from Census demographic and vehicle availability tables. Used as the primary operational indicator of local transit dependency.
  • Regional Transit Need Category (need_cat): Operationalized tercile categorization of Pct_ZeroCar across the tri-county region, grouped into Low (\(1\)), Moderate (\(2\)), and High (\(3\)) relative transit need.
  • Regional Transit Service Tier (access_cat): Operationalized access tier reflecting either regional transit employment reach (D5CR) or pedestrian stop proximity (D4A), grouped into Low/Far (\(1\)), Moderate (\(2\)), and High/Close (\(3\)) relative supply.
  • Street Intersection Density (D3A): Density of multi-leg street intersections per square mile of unprotected land. Scale: Higher values reflect a tighter, more interconnected pedestrian street grid that minimizes walking detour distances.
  • Transit Bivariate Matrix Group (group): Combined 9-cell categorical grouping code (paste0(need_cat, “-”, access_cat)) cross-tabulating relative transit need against transit supply, identifying spatial equity alignment and critical transit deserts.
  • Transit Regional Job Accessibility (D5CR): Total regional employment accessible within a 45-minute commute via public transit. Captures the functional employment utility of the regional transit network beyond physical stop presence.
  • Jobs-to-Household Ratio (D2A_JPHH): Balance between local employment opportunities and resident households within the block group. Captures local land-use mix and proximity between trip origins and destinations.

Methods

1.4 Council bluffs - Omaha Comparisons

This analysis was comparing block groups across the Omaha-Council bluffs metropolitan region. Eight measures were extracted from the U.S.Environmental Protection Agency’s Smart Location Database (SLD Version 3.0) to capture key dimensions of urban form, pedestrian infrastructure, and transit accessibility. EPA measures were standardized with a 12-digit Census FiPS identifier and linked to 2010 cartographic shapefiles from the U.S. Census bureau using the tigris spatial library in R.

Environmental indicators in metropolitan areas typically exhibity skew and non normality due to jobs and housing being distributed unevenly within cities. Distribution traits were checked to determine degree of skew and kurtosis in order to identify if parametric test would be appropriate to use on the data.

As a result of this trend Omaha and Council bluffs were evaluated using a two-tailed Mann-Whitney U test based on median rather than sample means. To reduce type one error inflation from repeated hypothesis testing a Bonferroni adjustment was applied. In order to determine practical significance, an effect size of Cliff’s delta (\(\delta\)) was obtained along side 95% confidence intervals.

Bivariate Transit Equity and Spatial map Based on findings of the hypothesis testing a need to identify spatial mapping of differences between the two states was identified. The goal was to map transit need against existing transit supply with respect to the border between nebraska and Iowa. Two seperate variables were used as evidence of transit supply. Physical stop proximity (D4A) which looked at the physical distance to the nearest transit stop and system destination reach (D5CR) which measured the volume of jobs within a 45 minute transit trip. These produced a 9 cell matrix ranging from high transit service and high car ownership and transit deserts with low transit service and low car ownership.Because approximately half of regional block groups lack fixed-route transit coverage and share the 3,000-meter cap, stop proximity tiers were assigned by grouping all unserved block groups into the lowest access tier, with the remaining served block groups partitioned by median walking distance. These terciles were split with the lowest third being low car ownership. This was to determine if lack of transit stops or other factors explained the difference in transit access to jobs.

Is there differences in transit access between Omaha and Iowa metro areas and are they explained by typical urban population gradients or as a result of the boudary between the two states. Figure 1: Distribution profiles of 8 indicators between both Council Bluffs and Omaha to display skew and normality.

The distribution confirms severe skew and non-normality across all 8 indicators in both metro areas.This confirmed the need to use non-parametric measures.

Metropolitan Hypothesis tests

Hypothesis testing with a bonferroni correction identified several statistically significant differences between Omaha and Council Bluffs (Table 2). Only the ratio of jobs to households had no significant difference between the two states. Omaha had higher transit employment reach, distance to transit stops, walkability, and automobile job access. Council bluffs on the other hand had significantly lower percentage of households with 0 cars. This suggests that Omaha has greater transit access as well as ability to walk where Council bluffs is heavily reliant on car usage for transportation. A major takeaway of this is that while Council Bluffs has lower zero car households they also have less access via car to job opportunity. Additionally those that do not have a car live in transportation deserts due to council bluffs having longer distance to transit stops and lower access to jobs via transit.

Physical Transit Stop Proximity Role in Job Access

Figure 3: Walking proximity and percent of zero car households.

When comparing walking distance to transit stops with job reach shows that those in Council bluffs are denied access to jobs Several block groups within central Council bluffs that lack access to personal vehicles also have moderate to far distance to their nearest transit stop. While some of these neighborhoods have bus stops they are much further than areas within Central Omaha. This supports that those living in Council Bluffs have a higher barrier to transit than those living across the river in Douglas County.

#Limitations The distance to transit stop variables was given an artificial cap of 3000 meters. While this helped to prevent extreme outliers it may have omitted the extent of isolation for those living further than 3000 meters from a stop. Similarly the 45 minute commute ceiling may not capture a broader trend of individuals in council bluffs doing a commute to work everyday that is greater than this ceiling.
